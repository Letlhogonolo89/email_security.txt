1. Three things that should have alerted me to the fact that the email from the bank was not legitimate?

- The sender’s email address was suspicious and misspelled (“fruad_servces@bank.com” instead of an official bank address).
- The email created urgency by using words like “URGENT” and pressuring the user to act quickly without verifying the message.
- The email included a ZIP attachment asking the user to open forms, which is unusual for banks and a common method used in phishing attacks.

2. Do i think it is possible my computer is infected with malware based on the given email scenario? 

Yes, it is possible that the computer is infected with malware. Opening the ZIP folder and the PDF file may have triggered malicious software hidden inside the attachment. Cybercriminals often disguise malware as harmless documents. Some PDF files can contain malicious scripts or links that automatically download malware when opened. The computer becoming slower afterwards may also be a warning sign of malware infection, although it is not definite proof.

3. Training paragraph for fellow employees

Phishing is a type of cyberattack where criminals pretend to be trusted organisations or people in order to trick users into revealing sensitive information or opening dangerous files. Social engineering is the manipulation of people into making security mistakes, often by creating fear, urgency, or curiosity. Employees should always carefully examine emails before opening attachments or clicking links. Warning signs include spelling mistakes, suspicious sender addresses, urgent requests, unexpected attachments, and messages asking for personal or financial information. Never trust an email simply because it appears to come from a bank or company you recognise. Instead, verify requests by contacting the organisation directly using official contact details. Malware infections can occur when harmful attachments, links, or downloads are opened, even if they appear harmless. To reduce the risk of infection, employees should keep antivirus software updated, avoid downloading unknown files, and report suspicious emails to the IT department immediately. It is also important to use strong passwords and enable multi-factor authentication whenever possible. Taking a few extra moments to inspect emails carefully can prevent serious security breaches, financial losses, and data theft. Cybersecurity awareness is everyone’s responsibility, and following safe online practices helps protect both employees and the organisation.
